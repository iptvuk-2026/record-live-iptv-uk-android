How to Record Live IPTV UK Channels Directly on Your Android TV Box

Your Android TV box can work like a set-top recorder: save a programme while it airs, schedule one from the TV guide, or record a whole series.

Recording Turns a Box Into a Recorder

Live television waits for nobody. A match that starts while you are still at work, a film that airs past bedtime, a weekly show that clashes with another one — recording is the answer to all three. On an Android TV box, recording needs no extra hardware beyond a storage drive: the player app saves the stream to a file as it plays.

Recording is a feature of the player app, not of IPTV itself: your subscription delivers a live stream, and the app you watch with decides whether that stream can be saved. That is why the same subscription records happily in one player and shows no record button at all in another. Three things decide whether a recording works: a player that supports recording, somewhere to put the files, and a spare connection while it runs.

This guide covers all three on an Android TV box — the device recording works best on. It uses TiviMate as the main example, and covers IPTV Smarters Pro and recording on a computer as well. It also covers the honest limits: some channels are protected and cannot be recorded, and a recording uses one of your plan’s connections.

First, Do You Even Need to Record?

Recording, catch-up and on-demand solve different problems. Catch-up lets you replay a programme that already aired, usually for a day or two after broadcast, without saving anything yourself. On-demand is a library of films and series you can start at any time. Recording is the only one of the three that saves a specific future broadcast to your own drive, exactly as it aired.

Before setting anything up, check the catch-up archive on the channel you care about. If the programme is there, watch it from catch-up and skip recording entirely. The EPG TV guide usually shows which programmes carry catch-up. Recording earns its keep for a programme with no catch-up, a live event you want to keep, or a clash with another programme you are watching.

The Three Things a Recording Needs

Every successful recording setup has the same three parts, and a failure in any one of them is the usual reason a recording comes out empty. Get them in place first.

1. A player that records

On Android TV, TiviMate is the strongest choice: one-tap recording, scheduled recordings from the guide, custom lengths and series recording. Recording is a TiviMate Premium feature, bought through the TiviMate Companion app on a phone — the television itself cannot take the payment. The free version does not record at all. IPTV Smarters Pro also offers recording on Android TV, though its scheduling tools are simpler.

2. Somewhere to put the files

A recording is a video file, and video files are large. An hour of standard-definition television takes roughly half a gigabyte to a gigabyte; an hour in high definition takes roughly one and a half to three gigabytes; an hour in 4K takes several gigabytes more and needs a drive that can write at least 30 megabytes per second. Internal storage on most Android TV boxes is small — often 8 or 16 gigabytes — so it fills after a handful of recordings. A USB drive plugged into the box is the simplest answer, and a shared folder on a computer or network drive works too. Set it as the recording folder in the player’s settings before the first recording.

3. A spare connection

A recording opens its own stream to the server, so it counts as one connection while it runs. Recording one channel while watching another means two streams at once, so your plan has to allow both. Check your plan’s connection limit on the pricing page before scheduling anything.

Setting Up Recording in TiviMate

TiviMate is the recording example here because its tools are the most complete. The setup takes a few minutes and only needs doing once; after that, recordings happen from the TV guide.

  1. Install TiviMate and add your playlist. Install TiviMate IPTV Player from Google Play, then add your playlist with the same Xtream Codes or M3U playlist details you use elsewhere. Let the channel list and guide load fully first; a half-loaded guide schedules recordings at the wrong times.
  2. Buy or trial TiviMate Premium on your phone. Install the TiviMate Companion app on a phone, buy Premium there or start its trial, then sign in to the same TiviMate account on the television.
  3. Connect the storage. Plug a USB drive into the box, or have the address, username and password of a network folder ready. On a stick-style device without a USB port, an OTG adapter is needed. Check the box can see the drive in its storage settings before opening TiviMate.
  4. Choose where recordings go. In TiviMate, open Settings, find the recording section, and point the recording folder at the USB drive or network folder — not internal storage. Set the padding too: a few minutes before the listed start and a generous margin after the end, because live broadcasts rarely keep to the listed second.
  5. Test with a short recording. Open a channel that is playing smoothly, hold OK on it in the guide and choose Record, then stop it after a minute and play the file back from the Recordings section. A recording keeps every freeze the live stream has.

Recording From the TV Guide

Once setup is done, the guide is where recording lives. Open the TV guide, move to a programme that has not started yet, select it and choose Record; it is marked as scheduled. To record what is on now, use the same option on the programme currently airing. For a weekly programme, choose the series option and TiviMate records each episode with the same title.

Three habits make scheduled recordings reliable. First, keep the guide accurate: recordings start and stop by the listings, so a guide that is an hour out records the wrong hour. If the EPG is not updating, fix that before scheduling anything. Second, do not fully power off the box: recordings need it awake, and power-saving settings can stop a recording if the device sleeps. Third, use a wired connection where you can. Finished recordings appear in the Recordings section, where you can play them with pause, rewind and fast-forward, or delete them to free space.

Recording in Other Players

TiviMate is not the only option. The steps are similar everywhere — set the folder, press record — but the limits differ, so pick the player by what it can actually do.

IPTV Smarters Pro

Recording is available from the in-player controls on Android TV: open the channel, bring up the playback controls and choose the record option. Scheduling is more basic than TiviMate’s.

XCIPTV and IBO Player

Both are solid players for watching, but their recording support is limited or absent depending on the version. Do not assume a player records just because it plays well — look for a recording section in its settings first.

Whatever player you use, the recording is a plain video file on your storage. Delete recordings you have watched — a drive that filled up silently is the most common reason a scheduled recording fails.

How Much Space and Speed Do You Need?

Storage is the part people underestimate. The table below is a rough guide — the exact size depends on the bitrate of the source stream — but it is close enough to plan a drive around.

Picture qualityRough size per hourWhat it suits
Standard definitionAbout 0.5–1 GBOlder shows
High definition (1080p)About 1.5–3 GBMost viewing
Full HD, high bitrateAbout 3–5 GBSport and action
4KAbout 7 GB or moreFilms and live events

Work backwards from that table. A 64 GB USB drive holds roughly twenty to forty hours of high-definition recording; a 128 GB drive doubles that, and costs little more. Buy a drive from a known brand and check its write speed — for smooth 4K recording the drive needs to sustain at least 30 megabytes per second. A wired Ethernet connection helps: Wi-Fi dropouts during a recording become permanent gaps in the file. Close other apps before a scheduled recording, and keep at least a gigabyte of internal storage free.

When a Recording Fails

Most failed recordings have one of a handful of causes, and they are quick to tell apart once you know what to look for.

There is no record button

Either the player does not record, or recording is a paid feature you have not unlocked. In TiviMate, that means Premium is not active on the account signed in on the television — check the Companion app on your phone. In another player, check its settings for a recording section; if there is none, the player does not record.

The recording is empty or will not play

This usually means the stream itself failed while recording — the channel froze, the service was not working at that hour, or the box went to sleep. A recording made from a freezing stream is a frozen file; fix the stream first, then record again.

The recording is the wrong programme

The guide was wrong: the EPG times were an hour out, or a live event overran into the next slot. Fix the guide first, then add padding to future recordings — a few minutes before and a generous margin after, especially for live sport. If the channels are not loading in the guide at all, the playlist itself needs attention first.

The app says the stream is protected

That channel blocks recording, and no setting on the box changes it. Use catch-up or on-demand for that programme instead. If a login problem appears at the same time, fix the login first — a recording cannot start on a playlist the player cannot open.

Recording Questions, Answered

Can I record on any Android TV box?

Almost any box that runs Android TV or Google TV and can install TiviMate or IPTV Smarters Pro can record, provided it has a USB port or a network folder for storage and a Premium recording feature where one is required. The Android TV guide covers which devices run which players; many smart TV apps cannot record at all.

Can I record two channels at once?

Only if your plan allows two connections at the same time, and only if the player supports it — TiviMate can record one channel while you watch another, which is two streams. Check the pricing page for the connection limit on your plan.

Is it legal to record IPTV for myself?

Recording a programme to watch later for your own personal use is the same idea as the video recorders households have used for decades, but the rules depend on what you record, where you are, and the terms of the service and the channel. The Verdict: Record What Catch-Up Cannot Cover

Recording on an Android TV box is straightforward once the three parts are in place: a player that records, a drive with room, and a spare connection. TiviMate Premium and a USB drive cover most households.

Use it where it earns its keep — programmes with no catch-up, live events worth keeping, and clashes you cannot watch twice — and let catch-up handle the rest. Set it up once, test it with a short recording, and check the Recordings section the morning after the first scheduled one.
